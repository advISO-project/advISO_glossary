Contributing to the Glossary
============================

The glossary is a collaborative effort, and we welcome contributions from the community. If you would like to contribute, please follow the guidelines below.

All contributions are made through `GitHub issues <https://github.com/advISO-project/advISO_glossary/issues>`_, so you will need a free `GitHub account <https://github.com/signup>`_. Each proposal is reviewed by the advISO team, who may suggest edits to the wording before it is published. You do not need to edit any files or submit code yourself.

Before you start, please:

* Search the :ref:`glossary <glossary>` to check the term is not already included.
* Check the `open issues <https://github.com/advISO-project/advISO_glossary/issues>`_ to see whether the term or change has already been proposed.

--------------------------------------------------------

Adding a new term
-----------------

To propose a new term, open an issue using the **📖 Propose a new glossary entry** template.

Each glossary entry follows the same structure, and the template will ask you for:

* **Term**, including any common abbreviation, e.g. *Measurement uncertainty (MU)*.
* **Source standard(s)**, with clause numbers where possible, e.g. *ISO 15189:2022 clause 7.3.4*.
* **Definition**, describing the term as it is used in ISO 15189 / ISO 17025 and the wider laboratory setting.
* **Example usage (wet-lab)**, a single sentence showing the term used in a laboratory context.
* **Bioinformatics translation**, explaining how the term applies to pipelines, software, reference data or compute infrastructure. If the meaning does not change, the entry will read "As above".
* **Example usage (bioinformatics)**, a single sentence showing the term used in a bioinformatics context.
* **Ontological references** (optional), matching terms from ontologies such as MeSH, NCIT, OBI or SIO. Useful places to search are the `EBI Ontology Lookup Service <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ols4/>`_ and the `MeSH Browser <https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/>`_.
* **Related glossary terms** (optional), existing entries the new term should cross-reference or be distinguished from.
* **Why the term should be added** (optional), e.g. a common source of confusion between wet-lab and bioinformatics teams.

Don't worry if you cannot complete every section. A partial proposal is still useful, and the advISO team can help fill in the gaps.

--------------------------------------------------------

Updating an existing term
-------------------------

To suggest a correction, clarification or update to an existing entry, open an issue using the **✏️ Propose change to an existing glossary term** template.

Changes might include:

* Correcting a factual error or inaccuracy.
* Clarifying wording that is unclear or ambiguous.
* Updating an entry to reflect a newer version of a standard or current practice.
* Adding a missing bioinformatics translation, example or ontological reference.
* Fixing typos, spelling or formatting.
* Removing a term, or merging it with another.

The template will ask you to name the term, say what type of change it is and which part of the entry is affected, copy the current text, give your proposed text, and explain the reason for the change. Where a change affects a definition, please cite the relevant standard or publication in your reason, as this makes review much quicker.

--------------------------------------------------------

Writing guidance
----------------

When proposing new or updated content, please:

* **Write in your own words.** ISO standards are copyrighted, so definitions should be paraphrased rather than copied verbatim.
* **Keep it concise.** Aim for a definition of one or two sentences and a single-sentence example.
* **Make examples realistic.** Examples drawn from clinical or public health genomics, such as pathogen sequencing, variant calling or QC, work best.

--------------------------------------------------------

What happens next?
------------------

#. The advISO team will review your issue and may ask questions or suggest edits in the comments.
#. Once agreed, the term will be added or updated in the glossary.
#. The issue will be closed and linked to the release that includes the change.

Thank you for helping to make ISO terminology clearer for the bioinformatics community!
